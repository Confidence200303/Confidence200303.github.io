### Sprint Outcome

The core objective was to prepare a structured onboarding playbook covering the main onboarding requirements and the four Zuke user categories. The playbook was written and reviewed or approved by my supervisor.

Optional visual enhancements, such as screenshots and a workflow diagram, were treated separately because they were not essential to documenting the onboarding process and may be subject to confidentiality restrictions.

This retrospective sprint backlog demonstrates how the work can be divided into manageable tasks, linked to product backlog items and assessed according to the intended outcome.

## Reflection

### 1. How accurate were my estimates?

I did not record formal time estimates for each task during the original work. Reconstructing the sprint backlog helped me recognise that future projects would benefit from estimating tasks before starting and recording the actual time taken. This would make it easier to monitor progress and improve future planning.

### 2. What blocked me, and how did I handle it?

Confidentiality and restricted access to client information limited the screenshots I could use as evidence. I addressed this by focusing on the documentation I could describe and share safely, rather than exposing private platform information. Another consideration was ensuring that the playbook reflected the requirements communicated by the team.

### 3. What would I change when planning the next sprint?

In future, I would define a clear sprint goal, estimate each task, establish acceptance criteria and record progress throughout the sprint. I would also identify dependencies, such as supervisor feedback and permission to use supporting evidence, before planning the work. This would improve task tracking, help identify blockers earlier and make the final outcome easier to evaluate.
