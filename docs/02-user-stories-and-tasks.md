# 02. User Stories and Tasks: Platform Ready Onboarding Playbook (Zuke)

This document outlines the user stories, acceptance criteria and task breakdowns for the Platform Ready Onboarding Playbook developed during my industry placement at Zuke through the CAPACITI Work-Integrated Learning (WIL) programme. It demonstrates how business onboarding needs can be translated into structured requirements, user-focused documentation and technical considerations.

**Approach:** Retrospective Agile analysis of the work completed and related activities undertaken during my placement.

---

## User Story 1: Business Onboarding Workflow

- **User Story ID:** US-01
- **As a:** New Zuke business user
- **I want to:** Follow a structured onboarding workflow
- **So that:** I can understand the information and steps required to prepare my business for the platform.

### Acceptance Criteria

1. The onboarding workflow identifies the information a business needs to prepare.
2. The onboarding steps are organised in a logical sequence from preparation to registration.
3. Each step describes a clear action or requirement.
4. The instructions are understandable to a new user without exposing confidential business information.

### Task Breakdown

- **T-01.1:** Analyse the onboarding requirements communicated by the Zuke team.
- **T-01.2:** Organise the playbook into logical onboarding sections.
- **T-01.3:** Write the onboarding instructions and document the relevant requirements.
- **T-01.4:** Review the document for clarity, consistency and completeness.

---

## User Story 2: User-Category-Specific Requirements

- **User Story ID:** US-02
- **As a:** Zuke user in the Ideator, Hustler, SME or Enterprise category
- **I want to:** Access onboarding guidance relevant to my business category
- **So that:** I can understand the requirements and support applicable to my business.

### Acceptance Criteria

1. The playbook identifies all four user categories: Ideators, Hustlers, SMEs and Enterprise.
2. The content distinguishes general onboarding requirements from category-specific guidance where applicable.
3. The guidance reflects the requirements communicated by the Zuke team.
4. Users can identify the sections relevant to their category.

### Task Breakdown

- **T-02.1:** Identify the four user categories addressed by the playbook.
- **T-02.2:** Organise the relevant content according to the intended user categories.
- **T-02.3:** Write the category-specific guidance.
- **T-02.4:** Review the sections for relevance, clarity and consistency with the onboarding requirements.

## User Story 3: Business Profile and Email Configuration

- **User Story ID:** US-03
- **As a:** Business owner preparing to join Zuke
- **I want to:** Understand how to prepare my business information, business email and email signature
- **So that:** I can present my business professionally and prepare its digital communication for onboarding.

### Acceptance Criteria

1. The playbook identifies the relevant business information required during onboarding.
2. The email setup and email signature requirements are explained.
3. The instructions describe the relevant preparation or configuration steps.
4. The guidance is understandable to users with different levels of technical experience.

### Task Breakdown

- **T-03.1:** Identify the business information required for onboarding.
- **T-03.2:** Document the relevant business email and email signature requirements.
- **T-03.3:** Organise the instructions into clear, practical sections.
- **T-03.4:** Review the guidance for clarity and alignment with the onboarding process.

## User Story 4: AI-Assisted Social Media Content Creation

- **User Story ID:** US-04
- **As a:** Business owner using Zuke's AI-assisted content tools
- **I want to:** Generate promotional content relevant to my products or services
- **So that:** I can communicate product benefits and promote my business on social media.

### Acceptance Criteria

1. The user can identify the product or service they want to promote.
2. The AI tool can generate content relevant to the selected product or service.
3. The generated content can be reviewed and adapted before publication.
4. The user understands how AI-assisted content creation can support their online presence.

### Task Breakdown

- **T-04.1:** Demonstrate how business users can access and use the AI-assisted content-generation tool.
- **T-04.2:** Guide users through generating promotional content for a specific product or service.
- **T-04.3:** Explain how the generated content can be adapted to the business and its intended audience.
- **T-04.4:** Help users understand how the tool can support their social media marketing activities.

**Technical relevance:** This activity formed part of my wider Zuke placement experience. It provided practical exposure to AI tool adoption, user interaction with content-generation systems and the importance of relevant inputs when generating useful output.

## User Story 5: Platform Registration and User Journey

- **User Story ID:** US-05
- **As a:** Business owner accessing the Zuke platform
- **I want to:** Follow clear registration instructions and navigate an understandable login journey
- **So that:** I can access the platform with minimal confusion.

### Acceptance Criteria

1. The registration instructions reflect the process communicated by the team.
2. The steps required to access the platform are easy to understand.
3. Potential usability issues in the login journey can be identified and explored.
4. Proposed interface changes are reviewed before implementation in the existing system.

### Task Breakdown

- **T-05.1:** Document the relevant platform registration guidance.
- **T-05.2:** Explore a proposed login-page concept to consider improvements to the user journey.
- **T-05.3:** Consider usability and the potential impact of interface changes on the existing platform.
- **T-05.4:** Identify the need for further planning and approval before implementing changes.

**Outcome:** The login-page concept remained exploratory and was not implemented. This activity provided exposure to usability considerations, frontend design exploration and the planning required before changing an existing system.

## User Story 6: API Integration Between Mailgun and Zoho

- **User Story ID:** US-06
- **As a:** Platform team member working on service integration
- **I want to:** Configure a connection between Mailgun and Zoho using an API key
- **So that:** The relevant services can communicate as intended.

### Acceptance Criteria

1. An API key is created through the appropriate service.
2. The key is configured in the intended integration environment.
3. The connection can be checked to determine whether the integration works as expected.
4. API credentials are handled securely and are not exposed in screenshots or documentation.

### Task Breakdown

- **T-06.1:** Create an API key in Mailgun.
- **T-06.2:** Work on connecting the Mailgun API key to the Zoho platform.
- **T-06.3:** Determine whether the integration has been successfully configured and tested.
- **T-06.4:** Ensure that API keys and other confidential configuration details are excluded from portfolio evidence.

**Evidence and outcome:** This activity provided practical exposure to API credentials and third-party service integration. Creating an API key does not, by itself, confirm that the integration was completed successfully. The final outcome should be recorded according to what was actually achieved.

## Reflection

### 1. How did thinking from the user's point of view change how I wrote the playbook?

Thinking from the user's point of view helped me translate onboarding requirements into practical instructions. I considered how businesses would navigate the process, what information they needed at each stage and how to make technical requirements understandable to users with different levels of digital experience. This encouraged me to focus on usability, information structure and the user journey rather than simply listing requirements.

### 2. Which story was hardest to define, and why?

The user-category story was challenging because the playbook needed to address Ideators, Hustlers, SMEs and Enterprise users without making the information confusing or repetitive. The technical stories also highlighted that activities such as exploring a login-page concept or creating an API key do not automatically mean a change has been implemented or an integration has succeeded. Each activity needs clear acceptance criteria and an accurate record of its outcome.

### 3. What did breaking stories into tasks help me with?

Breaking user stories into smaller tasks helped me separate user requirements from the work needed to address them. It made the activities easier to organise and helped me identify areas such as requirements analysis, documentation, AI tool guidance, usability exploration and API configuration. This strengthened my understanding of how Agile practices can support both technical development and work involving an existing platform.

It also demonstrated the importance of acceptance criteria: a task should be evaluated against an expected outcome rather than being considered complete simply because work has started.
